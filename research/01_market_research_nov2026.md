# Circus Bridge: Market Research & Strategy Reset
*Prepared 13 Nov 2026. Desk research plus industry judgement. Items marked ⚠️ need verification before you act on them.*

---

## 0. The short version

1. **Ethiopian acts are already for sale in the global market, but through other people's agencies.** Ukrainian aggregator Rising Stars lists acrobatic groups from Addis Ababa and Mekelle, one with MSC Divina / MSC Fantasia cruise credits. Kenyan and Tanzanian troupes dominate the "African acrobats" category, citing UAE, Turkey, Oman, MSC ships and Chinese theme parks. So the market *does* buy African circus. The open question is what Circus Bridge offers that those channels don't.
2. **The fastest path to a first contract is selling to agencies, not end buyers.** Cruise entertainment directors and big Gulf producers mostly book through trusted agencies and production houses. A three-person, eight-month-old agency won't get through to them directly in the next 60 days. The agencies that supply them are constantly hunting for acts, and their castings are public. **Pitch Circus Bridge as their vetted Ethiopia supply partner.**
3. **The current roster doesn't match what's in demand.** Yoni (juggling), Nahom (balance) and Nati (ladder/juggling) are all solo ground-and-object acts. The open castings are mostly for **duos (hand-to-hand, adagio, aerial), aerialists, and multi-person acrobatic troupes** ("African acrobats", tumbling, Icarian, teeterboard). Ethiopia's best-known international product is ensemble acrobatics: Circus Abyssinia's Konjowoch troupe went from the Edinburgh Fringe to London's Southbank and world tours. **You need at least one troupe product and one duo.**
4. **We're in the Gulf peak season right now, and cruise and resort casting for summer 2027 opens in the next 8–12 weeks.** This 60-day window is mainly for **getting ready and getting into agencies' files**, with a realistic shot at short Gulf or local gigs.
5. **The biggest operational risk is visas and mobility, not marketing.** Plan the business around markets Ethiopian passport holders can actually reach, and sell your paperwork competence as part of the product.

## 1. How this market actually works

### 1.1 The buying chain
```
End buyer                       Intermediary (who actually picks acts)        Supply
─────────────────────           ─────────────────────────────────────        ──────────────
Cruise line ent. dept   ◄────   Production cos (WEP, NCL Productions,  ◄────  Agencies / reps
                                Stiletto) + cruise agents (PEEL,              (Rising Stars,
                                Tim Raffles, Circus Guide, Rising Stars)      Circus Guide…)
Gulf govt seasons / malls ◄──   Event producers (Flash, Done, etc.)    ◄────   ◄── Circus Bridge
Hotels & resorts (TUI etc) ◄─   Animation cos (Wings, Bassikom…)       ◄────
Weddings / corporate     ◄──   Local ent. agencies (Bella, Scarlett…)  ◄────
```
- Cruise performers usually sign **an employment contract with the cruise line or producer**, plus a **separate commission contract with their agent**. Some agents only work with particular cruise lines.
- Entertainment directors get flooded with reels. A submission from an agent they know does better than a cold submission.
- At the top of the chain, the industry meets at **Seatrade Cruise Global, 5–8 April 2027, Miami Beach**.

**Implication:** Circus Bridge can make money at two levels:
- **Wholesale.** Supply acts to other agencies at a net fee, either sharing commission or earning on the artist's side. Lower margin, much faster.
- **Retail.** Sell directly to end buyers at 10–20% commission. Higher margin, takes 6–18 months of trust-building.

**Do both, but lead with wholesale.** The first signed contract will almost certainly come through an intermediary.

### 1.2 What buyers are casting for right now (late 2026 listings)
- **Cruise variety and circus:** acrobats, *"African acrobats"*, hand-to-hand, adagio duo, tumbling, Icarian/Risley, teeterboard, trampoline, Russian swing/bar, jumping stilts (one cruise casting listed all of these).
- **Celebrity Cruises productions:** juggling, street entertainment, Chinese pole, free-standing hoop or **ladder** acts, handstand, Cyr wheel, rolla bolla. → **Nati (ladder), Nahom (rolla-bolla-style board-and-ball balance) and Yoni (juggling) all fit this profile.** Worth targeting directly.
- **Guest acts** need a **self-contained show**, usually 1–2 × 45 minutes, or 45 + 20.
- **Turkey and Egypt resort season (April–October):** jugglers, clowns, stilt walkers, unicyclists, acrobats. Up to 2 hours of work a day, with flights, room and meals covered. Agencies like Rising Stars run "50+ performers each season" in Turkey.
- **Aerial is everywhere:** solos, duos, female aerialists with 2–3 acts. Ethiopia has few trained aerialists. That's a gap and a recruiting target.

### 1.3 Price benchmarks (indicative; most listings hide pay)
| Context | Figure found |
|---|---|
| Cruise guest entertainer (1–2 theatre shows/week) | ~$1,500–2,500+ per week |
| Cruise ensemble aerial duo (MSC, 2026) | €3,240–3,600 per month |
| Solo aerial, cruise (agency listing) | from €675 per week per performer |
| Dubai corporate magician (comparable variety act) | AED 1,500–6,000 per gig; brand launches AED 5,000–25,000 |
| India destination weddings, international acts | from ~₹2 lakh (about $2,400) for smaller acts |
| Agent commission norm | 10–15%, up to 20% for non-union variety work |

**Positioning:** don't compete with Kenyan or Tanzanian troupes on price. Compete on **reliability, video quality, a professional point of contact, fast replies, and visas handled**. Agencies will pay for less hassle.

**Honest takeaway:** "First dedicated agency" is not a safe claim. Drop it. *"Addis-based, curated, artist-first representation"* is true and harder to copy.

## 3. Mobility: the constraint that shapes everything

**What the research shows:**
- **Europe and UK:** African artists face high Schengen and UK visa refusal rates. One survey collected 435+ refused Schengen cases for African artists and culture professionals since 2019. The UK route for tours is the **Creative Worker visa**, which needs a **UK sponsor holding a licence**. Circus performers are exempt from the Equity minimum-pay rule, and the fee is £340 from April 2026. Short one-off gigs go through the visitor / Permitted Paid Engagement rules (⚠️ current status unclear: check gov.uk). UK processing can take **a month or more**.
- **UAE:** I found **no current ban on Ethiopian nationals** (the September 2025 suspension covered 9 other countries). An older UAE–Ethiopia labour-recruitment freeze existed. ⚠️ Confirm current rules for *entertainment* entry permits with a UAE event producer.
- **Cruise:** ⚠️ to verify for each line: seafarer medical, STCW basic safety, and for US ports a US C1/D visa (an interview at the US embassy in Addis). Usually the cruise line or producer guides this, but it adds 4–10 weeks.
- **Ethiopian side:** ⚠️ Ethiopia's **Overseas Employment Proclamation** regulates sending workers abroad. **Get a lawyer's opinion on whether placing performers abroad on contracts needs a licence or registration.** This is the item most likely to bite us later if we ignore it.

**What to do about it:**
1. **Build a "travel-ready" file for every artist:** passport with 18+ months' validity, travel history, past contracts and invitation letters, a bank statement, yellow-fever card, police clearance, and a professional bio letter.
2. **Sell markets in order of reachability:** Gulf (UAE, Saudi, Qatar, Oman) → East Africa and Indian Ocean resorts → India → China and Asia theme parks → Turkey and Egypt resorts → cruise → UK and Schengen.
3. **Use Ethiopian Airlines** as a selling point. Addis is Africa's biggest hub, with direct flights to the Gulf, India, China and Europe, which means cheap, fast deployment.
4. **Prepare for buyers' unspoken fear: performers absconding** (artists not returning home after a contract). European and Gulf buyers worry about this with African troupes. Address it professionally through contracted management, return-flight logistics, track record and references. Don't ignore it.

## 6. Product: what we actually sell (proposed)

Agencies buy **products**, not people. Package the roster:

| Product | Format | Who it's for | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solo variety acts:** Yoni (juggling/hats), Nahom (balance), Nati (ladder/juggling) | 5–8 min act; plus a 20–30 min solo set | Cruise productions, resorts, galas | Exists. Needs act videos and a tech rider. |
| **"Three-act Variety Set"** (Yoni + Nahom + Nati) | 20–30 min combined, plus roaming/walkabout | Gulf events, hotels, weddings | Can be built now with existing artists. |
| **Ethiopian Acrobatic Troupe** (6–10 performers: tumbling, human pyramids, chair stacking, contortion, Icarian) | 7-min act + 30–45-min full show | Cruise, Gulf seasons, theme parks, China, festivals | **Recruit or partner.** This is the flagship. |
| **Duo** (hand-to-hand / adagio / aerial) | 6–8 min | Cruise and resort castings | **Recruit.** |
| **Roaming and walkabout** (juggling, stilts, costumed characters) | 2–3 × 30 min sets | Malls, Riyadh/Dubai seasons, launches | Low cost to add. Yoni and Nati can do this now. |

**Before contacting anyone, the sales kit needs:**
1. **Master showreel, 90–120 seconds**, plus a **full unedited act video for each act**. Agencies ask for both, and some want footage filmed in the last 6 months.
2. **One-page act sheet per act:** duration, number of performers, stage size, **ceiling height**, rigging, floor surface, music, costume changes, languages.
3. **Rate card:** a *net* rate for agencies and a *retail* rate for direct clients, per show, per week and per month.
4. **Availability and passport matrix:** who can travel where, and when.
5. **Agency partner one-pager** (a PDF, separate from the client capability deck).
6. **Paperwork:** an artist representation agreement (exclusive per territory, transparent commission), a booking contract template, and a deposit/cancellation policy. Lawyer-reviewed.

## 7. Things you didn't mention but a professional would check

1. **Legal entity and licensing.** Is Circus Bridge registered (trade licence, TIN)? See §3 on the overseas employment law. Buyers and agencies will ask for an invoice from a registered company.
2. **Getting paid.** Ethiopia floated the birr (July 2024), and directive FXD/04/2026 (Feb 2026) eased foreign-currency accounts and **advance payments**. Open a business FX account so you can receive deposits. Consider a payment route for foreign clients (Wise / Payoneer-style, ⚠️ check availability). **Deposits are how you avoid funding flights yourself.**
3. **Email domain credibility.** `circuscr@circusbridge.pro.et` reads as unusual to European and Gulf buyers. A `.com` (about $12/year) with a clean address like `booking@` will improve reply rates and deliverability. Keep `.pro.et` as a redirect.
4. **Artist exclusivity and retention.** Without signed representation agreements, artists you market can be booked around you, by an agency or by going direct. Sign agreements *before* pushing their videos to agencies.
5. **Insurance.** Public liability, plus personal accident cover for aerial and acrobatic acts. Many venues require it.
6. **Artist welfare as a brand.** Fair, transparent pay (versus the "pay back the company" model reported in Ethiopian circus) helps you recruit and protects your reputation.
7. **Instagram is credibility, not sales.** Buyers check you exist there, but they book through email, WhatsApp, casting platforms and referrals. Spend the time on direct outreach and LinkedIn, and use social media for proof.
8. **Real testimonials.** None exist. The fastest way to get the first ones is local Addis gigs plus a hosted showcase evening.
9. **Watch for exploitative "agencies"** that charge artists fees. Our policy should say no fees to artists, commission only, and that should be stated publicly.
